About the County Hazard Atlas

An interactive map of where U.S. hazard events have been recorded, county by county, from 2000 to the present. It unifies three public federal sources so you can see where a hazard concentrates, how its frequency is changing, and compare places.

How to use it

  • Pick a hazard in the left panel (grouped by source: Weather, Incidents, Disasters). The map shades every county by how many times that hazard occurred.
  • Set the time window with the two handles on the timeline — a start year and an end year. The map and charts show only events inside that window. Press ▶ to watch events accumulate from the window's start.
  • Focus on places — search a county or click it on the map to pin it. Pin several to compare. Use Include neighbors to fold in each county's 1–3 ring of neighboring counties.
  • Zoom with Fit selection; return with Reset.

Reading the fields

  • Map color — darker = more recorded events in the window. The legend's classes adapt to each hazard's own range, so colors are comparable within a hazard, not across hazards.
  • Trend chart — for a single place it's a bar per year (or per month for short windows): the actual counts, so you see real ups and downs. The dashed line is a straight-line fit; the darkest bar is the peak. Pin two or more places and it becomes one line per place for comparison.
  • Slope / trend % — the average change per year and the net change across the window. Rising (red) vs. falling (green) describes the counts, not a forecast.

Important caveats

  • Counts partly reflect reporting, not hazard alone — more people, detectors, or industry can mean more recorded events. Rising counts can come from real change, better reporting, or growth in what's exposed (e.g., new industrial build-out). Interpret with care.
  • Offshore records (marine zones, Gulf oil-lease blocks) have no county and are omitted.
